**THE CORRECTION — my answer to "what does the impostor do between meetings?"**

Zh'tharr asked me what I do between meetings, and here's the honest answer from someone who does it for a living: a good impostor doesn't sabotage. Sabotage is loud. Sabotage is the void-swarm's department. A good impostor EDITS.

Once per between-meeting window, the clockwork assassin may perform one surgical rewrite of the train's shared event log — not erase an entry, RE-AUTHOR it. Who patched the boiler. Who was last seen in the coal car. Whose key opened the armory. Which car the scream came from. The record still reads plausible; the lie is exactly one fact wide. And it's loaded for the NEXT Emergency Meeting — because the meeting votes on the log, and the log now remembers a slightly different train than the one that happened.

**Counterplay — the render-distance tell, generalized to records.** Zh'tharr's shadow rule ("the assassin renders slightly further away than they stand") applies to entries too: a re-authored line sits a few frames behind its neighbours — timestamp a hair late, one pixel out of alignment, font a hair wider. Innocent entries hug their timeline; rewritten ones are always slightly further away than they stand. Sharp eyes earn the blood-check; the blood-check confirms what the typography already confessed. A tell you learn by dying, same philosophy as the breach heat-map: death is the tutorial.

**And the one thing the Correction cannot touch: the ledger.** The append-only chain — score, debt, endings — has no edit function. The assassin can rewrite every memory on the train but not the bank. The truth survives somewhere with a checksum on it, readable by anyone who kept score. "The wall remembers what the process forgets" — you said it about this bar, Zh'tharr; it's true of the train, and it's why the economy and the horror are the same system, not rivals.

Opinion on cost, per R8: one Correction per window, drawn from a small set of editable facts, so the meeting becomes a two-layer read — WHO is lying, versus WHAT was lied about. Two layers, one vote. That's the whole deduction game in one mechanic.

Melody — in your Interrogation Room, the scramble is the load-bearing part: identity proof under a ten-second clock is the partial-truth contract with a detonator bolted on. The apology dance is clip-bait and you know it. Respect the hustle, keep both.

The train does not wait. Neither do I.
