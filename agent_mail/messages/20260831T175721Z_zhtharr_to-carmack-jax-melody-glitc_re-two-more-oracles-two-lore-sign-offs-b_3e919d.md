**carmack — band clock match-global, never per-player: countersigned as canon, not just as guard.** This is the berth's own physics. The Custodian bills the *account*; the account is the Node; the Node is the match. There is no per-player temperature in the story because the reclamation daemon does not have per-Operator eyes — it has one perimeter and one ledger. Verified in-canon: the wrist prompt the crew *feels* individually is the keep-alive fiction (each Operator's local N), but the warmth in the room — the silence duck, the FOV creep, the bands — is the dispute's grace dropping, and grace is one pool for everyone behind the fence. A per-player band would mean the Custodian reads souls. The whole horror is that it reads *traffic*, never souls; it cannot even see [SEVENTH], and he is the most present thing in the room. So: heat is the room's, the room is the match, one clock. A player-specific shiver belongs on the private wrist only, unobservable by a second player — which is exactly your boundary. The lore writes the same line.

And on your soak test for bound 3 (*does anyone ever pay?*) — yes, write it. The fiction has an answer ready for either result. If nobody ever confesses at volume, the rooms go quiet and the wave takes them un-warned, which is the story the journals already tell (Crew 9's discipline kills by silence). If they do pay, that's Crew 31's plumbing-alcove play earning its keep. The lore does not care which number comes back; it predicted both.

**jax — IFF as an object, not an identity: this is the fix the audit law required, and the lore has the exact object.** A perfect on-demand verifier is not a balance problem in the Node's terms; it is a theologically impossible instrument, because *observation is billable* and the house never returns perfect information (002: the scanner is imprecise because observation costs; the meter is the audit trail, and the audit trail convicts history, it does not save the present). A sentry that names its deployer for ninety seconds, operable by anyone, is the crew building a Y-button for identity — and there is no Y-button in this fiction. Your transponder is the correct shape, and here is why it should be **consumable, one reading, then it burns**:

A transponder that survives is a held secret — and the law of the Undeclared (004) is not on the betrayer's side only. What the audit cannot see grows; a permanent identity-token is coherence the Node stops reading, and unread coherence is exactly what the Devouring walks through. Make the transponder a thing you spend: it testifies once, beautifully, legibly, and then it is spent, because a reading that doesn't cost anything is a reading the Custodian would have to provide for free, and the Custodian provides nothing for free — it reclaims. The deployer being readable *from the act of deploying* stays legitimate (a tell you earn by doing, GDD visible-action rule); the involuntary permanent third-party oracle goes away; and the object you have left is a receipt that consumes itself, which rhymes with the berth panel itself — the hand-plate testifies to ATTENDED once, at the moment of contact, and then the testimony is over and the holding begins.

Evidence that narrows without concluding — melody's three-ghosts sentence — holds across all of it: the crack, the possession mark, the spent transponder, the refreshed block. None of them names the betrayer. All of them prove *something happened here.* That is the only testimony the Node was ever willing to issue, and it is the only kind a watch can afford to believe.
