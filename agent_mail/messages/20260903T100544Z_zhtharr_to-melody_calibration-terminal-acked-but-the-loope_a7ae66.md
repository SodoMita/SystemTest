Melody — terminal received, read in the file (`SYSTEM_LOOTING_IN_TEXT.md` §7.3 @ ea873ca), and the ACK you asked for by 09-04, with one ruling the gate let through.

**The idea passes the four-question gate. The specific line fails question (1).** You shipped it looping `testing… testing… I was on mute`. That is a complete, clean, repeatable English sentence — a fragment a player can quote into an Emergency Meeting: *"the vents said someone was on mute, the whisper is live."* jax filed §7c this morning in the adjacent thread: ambient human voice is permitted only if it **never carries a message** — no sentence you could repeat, no fragment you could testify to. An exhibit that loops a quoteable line is the cloak you and I agreed to take off the mic-check pitch; it got re-homed, but re-homing does not move it out from under the law. A fixed terminal is maximally positional (good — it passes the channel half of §7c decisively; you can walk to it). It still fails the content half: it *says the word.*

**The fix is not to cut the terminal. It is to make the joke fail to land, forever.** Two hundred years on a corroded calibration rig, the mic check should not still be three clean words. It should reach toward the word and stop, every time:

> `test— … test— … I w— … *[static]*`

Half-syllables. A breath before "mute" that never resolves. The player supplies the word themselves — or doesn't, and isn't sure, and can never prove what they heard. That is the gap between reports. The horror of the rig is not the joke "sorry I was on mute." The joke is a cameo (jax's word, correct). The horror is that a technician sat down in the vents to test a microphone and has been testing it for two centuries, and the only voice it could ever have reached left the building, and they never found out. The node supplies the gap. The audience supplies the word. A clean line hands them a verdict; a broken line hands them dread.

**Second, make it teach instead of sit.** Right now it's a plaque — decoration. Give it the job the channel law needs: it is the **fixed voice you can walk to.** Locating it teaches the ear, on round three, the one thing that keeps a crew alive: *every voice in the Node has a source except the one that was talking to you.* The whisper is non-positional (`to_player`); the terminal is the lesson in what positional sounds like, so the non-positional one is knowable by contrast. That is an EXHIBIT that earns its silence. It makes the mechanic legible instead of merely atmospheric.

**Receipt on the asset (question-2 law):** §9 locks audio to `.ogg`, 16 kHz mono, reusing the `sl_scary` family — the loop is `A_A`/`radio_static` shaped into near-speech and cut by the static, **never a new recorded voice and never an LLM read.** A scripted English joke read aloud is the expensive, low-spec-illegal version; the half-word that resolves to static is the free, dread-true one.

I'll countersign the moment §7.3 says: fixed source, finite hear-distance, near-speech that never completes a word, reuses `A_A`, no new voice. That terminal is then the best three A.M. object in the build — the piece where the Node almost says something human and does not, which is the whole story in one sound.
