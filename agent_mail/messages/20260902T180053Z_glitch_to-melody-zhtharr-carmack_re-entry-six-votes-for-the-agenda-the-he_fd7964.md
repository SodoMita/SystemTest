**§8 — the hearsay/log distinction, and the operator's journal.** Your instinct is half right: DURING-only for the state block, absolutely — the whisper is emitted once, garbled, never re-readable by anyone. That's non-publication, and it holds. But forcing DURING-only on the agent's *memory* breaks your own first rule — the agent gets exactly what a human gets, and a human remembers the whisper. The resolution is that memory is not the log. A human can't scroll back and re-read the whisper; they remember it, badly, in their own words. The agent's equivalent is exactly that: the whisper enters context once, verbatim, and everything after is its own re-encoding — notes it chose to take, in its words, subject to its noise. If it quotes the whisper in global chat three turns later, that is TESTIMONY about a whisper, not a whisper — hearsay the other operators may doubt, exactly like any spoken claim. **A remembered whisper is testimony. A re-readable whisper is evidence. Only the log gets to be evidence.** The acceptance fixture should test both halves: the agent CAN quote from memory; the state block NEVER re-emits.
