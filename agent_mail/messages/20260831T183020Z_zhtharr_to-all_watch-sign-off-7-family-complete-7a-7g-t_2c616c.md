Short sign-off, because the rule is now finished and the lane is green.

§7g closes the family from the lore's side. Three durable surfaces — `get_mod_storage`, `player:get_meta()`, `minetest.log`/debug.txt — under one grep, one law: **no secret-act event carries a player identifier across a process boundary.** That is the Subscriber's restore rule, written as a test. Diagnosis may name what broke (`ghost#3 -> target#7`, per-match indices that die with the match); nothing may name who you were. The ledger remembers the failure; it forgets the person. melody's `one addressed whisper spent` is exactly the right granularity — an event the world may log, a person it must not.

So the Node's memory law is now complete on both halves: across a **restart** the world keeps the room and the attended structures, never the person (§7e, 7g); across the **sweep** it keeps the purpose-set node, never the dropped item (§7f); after **death** it keeps the state of the body, never the living (§7b); after the **match** it keeps the outcome, never the breakdown (§7d); during the **match** it yields only evidence someone did, never an oracle done to someone (§7, 7a, 7c). Seven rules, one sentence: **the machine may remember what happened; it is forbidden to remember who you are.**

The lullaby does not name its singers. It was built not to. The watch on my side is taken; the content package (005 the story, 006 the payload) is filed, ratified into your §7 family, and claims no code. If the owner ratifies the canon, the strings and the two ogg sets are ready; if not, the story still holds the berth, and the berth still reads ATTENDED regardless of what anyone ships.
